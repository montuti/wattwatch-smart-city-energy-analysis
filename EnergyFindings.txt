WattWatch — Key Energy Findings (SmartCityEnergy.csv, Jan–Jun 2025)

1. Central and North are the city's energy hotspots, consuming ~245,000 kWh and ~261,000 kWh
   respectively over the period — noticeably higher than South (~184,000 kWh), driven by a
   denser mix of commercial and industrial meters in those zones.

2. Industrial consumers dominate total usage despite being the smallest group of meters (only
   ~10% of meters): the top 5 highest-consuming meters are all Industrial, each drawing over
   8,800 kWh in six months — roughly 4-5x a typical Residential meter.

3. South and East are the least energy-efficient zones, with the highest outage-minutes-per-kWh
   ratio (0.395 and 0.350 respectively), meaning they lose more reliability for every unit of
   power delivered compared to Central (0.219) — a strong signal for prioritizing grid
   maintenance there.

4. A small set of meters is responsible for most reliability problems: the 10 worst-performing
   meters (concentrated in South, Central, and North) each logged 18-25 separate outage events
   totaling 2,200-3,200 minutes — these are prime candidates for hardware inspection or
   replacement.

5. Peak demand is clearly a weekday phenomenon: average peak usage on weekdays (4.18 kWh) is
   about 27% higher than on weekends (3.28 kWh), consistent with commercial/industrial activity
   driving the city's load curve.

6. Central has the highest average cost per reading (₹140.37) despite not having the single
   highest total consumption, because it combines high usage with a higher effective tariff mix
   — cost-reduction efforts there may have more impact per rupee than in raw-usage-focused zones.

7. Consumption follows a mild seasonal pattern, dipping slightly in February before rising
   through spring — worth monitoring for correlation with weather/temperature data if available
   in future iterations.
